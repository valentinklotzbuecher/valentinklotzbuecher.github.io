Helpline research

NoteInteractive Figure: Conversation Topics in German Helpline Calls
  • TelefonSeelsorge
  • Nummer gegen Kummer · youth
  • Nummer gegen Kummer · parents

Deviation of conversation topics from their 2019 baseline, in percentage points, for calls to Germany’s two nationwide helplines for young people. Each tab uses that helpline’s own topic taxonomy rather than a harmonised one, so the categories differ between them: TelefonSeelsorge, the country’s largest helpline, records 41 topics for its adult callers, while Nummer gegen Kummer records 88 finer-grained ones, split here into its youth line (callers under 20) and its parent line (callers 20 and over).

Hover any line for the topic, the deviation that week and, for Nummer gegen Kummer, the broader category it belongs to; click a line to highlight it and name it above the plot. Based on some three million calls between January 2019 and May 2022.

NoteInteractive Figure: US Helpline Calls by State

Each grey line is one US state; the mauve line is the nationwide weekly average. Calls are shown as deviations from each state’s own average over the period, so states of very different size can share one scale, and the two helplines get their own panel because they swing by very different amounts. Hover any line to identify the state, or click it to highlight that state in both panels and name it above the plot. Source data for Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. 6, covering January 2020 to March 2021.

Political economy

NoteInteractive Figure: Terror Groups, 1860-1950

Each marker is one terror group, placed at the city where it formed; hover for its name, ideology and years of activity. The two social-revolutionary ideologies the paper is about — anarchist and leftist — are drawn in colour; the remaining ideologies recede into greys but are all still shown and named on hover. Drag to pan and use the buttons at the top right to zoom — useful for the dense European cluster. Groups from the same city are fanned out slightly so they stay distinguishable, and present-day borders are drawn for orientation only.

Group data are from J. Tschantret, “The old terrorism: a dataset, 1860–1969”, International Interactions 45(5): 933–948 (2019), as used in “Class warfare” below.
